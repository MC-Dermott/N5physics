How a linac works

  • Charged particles are accelerated along a straight line through a series of hollow metal drift tubes in a vacuum.
  • Alternate tubes are connected to opposite terminals of an alternating (AC) supply, so each gap between tubes has an electric field.
  • The particle gains energy W = QV every time it crosses a gap while the field points the right way.
  • Inside a tube there is no electric field, so the particle drifts at constant speed while the polarity swaps over.
  • The particle spends the same time (half a cycle) in every tube, but it is moving faster, so the tubes get longer: length = speed × time.

Check your understanding

1. Why must the tubes get longer along the linac?The particle gets faster each gap. The AC field reverses every half cycle, so the particle needs to take the same time (half a cycle) to cross each tube. Distance = speed × time, so a faster particle needs a longer tube.
2. Why is the particle not slowed down when the supply reverses?The particle is inside a metal tube. There is no electric field inside it, so the polarity can change without any force on the particle. It only feels a force in the gaps.
3. What goes wrong if the frequency of the supply is too high or too low?The particle reaches the gap when the field is not pointing in the direction of its motion. It gains less energy, or is even slowed down, so it does not reach the intended energy.
4. A proton crosses a 50 kV gap. How much kinetic energy does it gain?W = QV = 1.6 × 10-19 × 50 000 = 8.0 × 10-15 J (50 keV). Every gap gives the same energy gain, but speed does not increase by equal steps because Ek = ½mv2.
5. Give one limitation of a linac.To reach high energy it has to be very long (many tubes), which is expensive. Each particle passes through each gap only once.

How a cyclotron works

  • Two hollow, D-shaped metal boxes (dees) sit in a vacuum with a small gap between them. A uniform magnetic field acts over both dees.
  • An AC voltage is connected across the dees, so there is an electric field in the gap that reverses direction.
  • The proton is accelerated each time it crosses the gap (W = QV).
  • Inside a dee there is no electric field. The magnetic force is at right angles to the motion, so the proton moves in a semicircle at constant speed and comes back to the gap.
  • Faster protons move in bigger semicircles, so the path is a spiral that ends when the proton reaches the edge of the dees.
  • A faster proton has a bigger circle to travel, so the time for each half-circle stays the same and the AC frequency can stay fixed.

Extension (not needed at Higher): r = mv/qB and T = 2πm/qB. The speed cancels, which is why the time for each half-circle does not depend on the speed.

Things to try

  • Watch the graph of time for each half-circle. It stays flat even though the radius and the speed keep increasing.
  • Change the AC frequency away from the matching value. Why does the proton stop gaining energy?
  • Double the gap voltage. Does the final energy change? How many crossings does it take?
  • Increase the magnetic field. What happens to the energy at the edge of the dees?
  • Tick the relativity box: the mass increases at high speed. What happens to the time for each half-circle, and what happens to the energy gain?

Check your understanding

1. Why can the AC supply keep a fixed frequency even though the proton keeps speeding up?A faster proton moves in a bigger semicircle. It has further to go but is also moving faster, so the time for each half-circle is the same. The field in the gap therefore reverses at the right moment every time.
2. Why is there no electric field inside the dees?The metal dees shield the inside. The proton only gains energy in the gap. Inside the dee, the magnetic field makes it follow a semicircle at constant speed.
3. What is the job of the magnetic field, and does it change the proton's energy?The force on a moving charge in a magnetic field is at right angles to its velocity. It bends the path into a circle (so the proton comes back to the gap) but does no work, so the kinetic energy does not change in the dees.
4. Give one advantage of a cyclotron over a linac.The proton passes through the same gap many times, so the machine is much more compact than a linac of the same final energy.
5. Why can an ordinary cyclotron not reach very high energies?As the speed gets close to the speed of light the mass of the particle increases, so each half-circle takes longer. The particle arrives at the gap too late, the field no longer matches, and it stops being accelerated. Also, the magnet has to be bigger for higher energy. A synchrotron fixes this by changing the frequency and the magnetic field.

How a synchrotron works

  • Particles go round and round a ring of fixed size inside a vacuum pipe.
  • Bending magnets in the curved sections give a magnetic field. The force on a moving charge is at right angles to its motion, so the path is bent round the ring but the speed is not changed. There is no magnetic field in the straight sections.
  • In the straight sections, RF cavities give an electric field that accelerates the particle each time it passes through (W = QV). More cavities mean more energy gained per turn.
  • As the particle gets faster it needs a bigger sideways force to follow the same ring, so the magnetic field is increased in step with its momentum.
  • The particle goes round faster, so the frequency of the accelerating voltage must also increase to stay in step. Every time the particle reaches a cavity the field must be pointing the right way. That is why it is called a synchrotron.

Check your understanding

1. Why are magnets needed, and what do they do to the particle?A charged particle moving in a magnetic field experiences a force at right angles to its velocity. This bends its path round the ring but does not change its speed or energy. The electric field in the cavities is what speeds it up.
2. Why must the magnetic field get stronger as the particle gains energy?A faster (higher momentum) particle is harder to turn. To keep it going round the same ring, the sideways force must be bigger, so the magnetic field has to increase. If it did not, the path would curve less than the ring and the particle would hit the wall.
3. Why must the frequency of the accelerating voltage change?As the particle speeds up, it takes less time to go round the ring. The voltage must reverse in step with this, so that the particle arrives at each cavity when the field is in the direction to accelerate it.
4. Why does the frequency level off near the end, while the field B keeps rising?The speed approaches the speed of light but cannot reach it, so the time for one turn hardly changes any more. The particle's energy and momentum still rise, so B has to keep increasing.
5. Give one advantage of a synchrotron over a linac, and one over a cyclotron.Over a linac: the particles pass through the same cavities many times, so very high energies can be reached with much less equipment. Over a cyclotron: the particles stay on a ring of fixed size (only a ring of magnets is needed, not one huge magnet), and the changing frequency and field let it reach energies where the particle's mass has increased a lot.
